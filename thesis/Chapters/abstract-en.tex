Goiás and the Federal District (about 346,000~km\textsuperscript{2} of the Cerrado biome)
embody the central tension of land use in the tropics: expanding and intensifying production
without compromising the ecosystems and water resources it depends on. This dissertation builds an
\textbf{open multi-segment agricultural suitability atlas, with zoning and climate projection}, for
GO and DF at 250~m resolution, covering seven segments: soybean, sugarcane, other annual crops, fish
farming, livestock, conservation, and photovoltaic generation. Current suitability results from an
FAO-style multicriteria evaluation (fuzzy membership and AHP weights); zoning, from unsupervised
$k$-means clustering over a decorrelated representation of the variables; and the mid-century
projection, from the change-factor method applied to five CMIP6 models. All processing runs on Google
Earth Engine, from public data and \textbf{without exporting any external asset to the platform}, and
the outputs are validated against current land use (MapBiomas) and a productivity indicator (MODIS
MOD17). The territory splits into \textbf{four zones}: two with a defined vocation (the escarpment, for
conservation, and the drained lowland, for fish farming) and two, covering 74\% of the area, in which
the two best-placed segments are practically indistinguishable. Built solely from biophysical
variables, the zones separate observed productivity sharply ($F = 1{,}417$ on the half of the territory
reserved for validation), and the suitability surfaces reproduce the current geography of cropping
(AUC of 0.864 for soybean and 0.906 for sugarcane, also out of sample). The gap between suitability and
current use is large (44\% of the territory for other annual crops and 41\% for soybean), mostly on
pastureland, which points to intensification rather than expansion onto native vegetation. Projected
change is modest but consistent across models. It mainly affects \textbf{photovoltaic generation},
which loses more than half of its suitable area under the hottest scenario, followed by conservation
and the maize, cotton and sorghum group. The soybean and sugarcane plateaus remain stable, and
\textbf{fish farming is the only segment that gains suitable area}, as the thermal constraint relaxes.
The method can be reproduced in other Cerrado states at low marginal cost.
