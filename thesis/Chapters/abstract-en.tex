Goiás and the Federal District ($\sim$340,000~km\textsuperscript{2} of the Cerrado biome)
concentrate the central tension of land-use planning in the tropics: expanding and intensifying
production without compromising the ecosystems and water resources it depends on. This
dissertation builds, for GO~and~DF at 250~m, an \textbf{open multi-segment agricultural
suitability atlas with zoning and climate projection}, covering seven land-use segments:
soybean, sugarcane, other annual crops, aquaculture, livestock, forest/Cerrado
conservation, and photovoltaic generation. Current suitability results from an FAO-style
multicriteria evaluation (fuzzy membership and AHP weights). Agro-environmental zoning comes from
unsupervised k-means clustering over a decorrelated representation of the variables. And the
mid-century projection applies the change-factor method to an ensemble of five
CMIP6 models. All processing runs server-side, on Google Earth Engine, \textbf{without exporting
any external asset to the platform}, validated against current land use (MapBiomas) and a
productivity indicator (MODIS MOD17). The results answer the three research questions: current
suitability surfaces for the seven segments and a partition into \textbf{four zones}, two of them
with a defined comparative vocation --- the conservation escarpment and the drained lowland,
suited to aquaculture --- and two in which the two best-placed segments are practically
indistinguishable, together covering 74\% of the territory. The zones, built solely from
biophysical variables, separate observed productivity sharply ($F = 1417$ on the half of the
territory held out for validation), and the suitability surfaces reproduce the current geography
of cropping (soybean AUC of 0.864 and sugarcane AUC of 0.906, likewise out of sample). The work
further reveals a relevant gap between potential suitability and current use --- 44\% of the
territory for other annual crops and 41\% for soybean ---, especially in pastureland, pointing to
intensification, rather than expansion onto native vegetation, as the most likely direction of
land-use change in the region. Mid-century climate change is modest in magnitude, yet consistent
across the ensemble: it affects mainly \textbf{photovoltaic generation}, which loses more than
half of its suitable area under the hottest scenario, followed by conservation and the
maize/cotton/sorghum group, while the prime soybean and sugarcane plateaus remain essentially
stable. \textbf{Aquaculture is the only one of the seven segments that gains suitable area}, as
the thermal constraint relaxes. The method runs entirely in the cloud from public data, making it
reproducible at low marginal cost for other Cerrado states.
